## M1: Marketing in the Digital Era

### The Rise of DTC Brands
Direct to consumer (DTC) brands bypass traditional retail channels and sell products directly to consumers. Instead of advertising through television and print media, DTCs utilize digital and social marketing tools to acquire and engage consumers.

### Value Chain Analysis
DTC success comes from reinventing parts of the value chain. The five stages:

1. **Customer Insight** — DTC companies monitor actual purchase behavior (Google Trends, Amazon data, social media) rather than relying on surveys. Provides more accurate, real-time consumer preferences.
2. **R&D and Product Design** — DTCs focus on customer benefits over product features. Agile test-and-learn approach. Differentiation through mission, service, and responsiveness rather than proprietary tech.
3. **Production and Manufacturing** — Most DTCs outsource manufacturing (variable cost model, faster adaptation). Trade-off: less quality control vs. lower capital intensity.
4. **Marketing and Distribution** — Digital channels enable precise targeting and performance measurement. Key tension: performance marketing (short-term, measurable) vs. brand building (long-term, harder to measure). CAC has increased significantly over time.
5. **Customer Experience** — Direct consumer access enables rapid feedback loops, product refinement, and community building.

### DTC Marketing — Innovations vs. Challenges

| Innovations | Challenges |
|---|---|
| Lower barrier to entry vs. TV/print | Over-reliance on performance marketing at expense of brand |
| Precise targeting | Digital channels becoming more expensive |
| Performance marketing is measurable | Risk of losing synergy with offline channels |

### How Traditional Brands Respond
- **Acquire DTCs**: Access to customers and data, but integration risk and profitability challenges.
- **Build a DTC Brand in-house**: Faces cannibalization risk, resource misalignment, and growth-vs-profitability tension.
- **Adapt and Learn**: Combine DTC innovations (direct distribution, agile methods, digital insight) with existing strengths (brand awareness, scale, distribution).

## M2: Crafting a Digital Marketing Plan

### The Four Components of a Marketing Plan

| Component | Definition |
|---|---|
| Goals/Objectives | Broader outcomes you hope to achieve (increase brand awareness, double sales) |
| Target Audience | The group of consumers that will be the focus of your marketing campaign |
| Value Proposition | The key differentiation of your product or service; why consumers would buy from you over a competitor |
| Metrics | KPIs that track the success of your marketing efforts |

### Goals and Objectives — The Marketing Funnel

- **Awareness**: Top of funnel. Metrics: impressions, views. *Unaided awareness* = unprompted brand recall. *Aided awareness* = brand recognition when prompted.
- **Consideration**: Middle of funnel. Objectives: website traffic, store visits, content engagement. Consumers evaluate your brand vs. competition.
- **Conversion**: Bottom of funnel. Objective: converting engaged consumers into buyers. Metrics: sales, revenue.

### Segmentation — Three Approaches

- **Who** (Demographics): Age, income, gender, occupation, lifestyle, location. Most common approach.
- **What** (Behavioral): Segment based on customer behavior related to your product.
- **Why** (Motivational/Psychographic): Why they buy — convenience, value, status, etc.

### Value Proposition

The unique value or benefits your product provides that distinguishes you from competition. Determines how consumers think of your brand relative to competitors.

**Writing a Positioning Statement** — four elements:
1. Define your target audience whose needs you aim to serve
2. Articulate the unique value you provide to consumers
3. Define the competitive set (frame of reference for purchase decisions)
4. Provide reasons to believe your brand's claim with supporting evidence

### The Three Cs of Brand Positioning

- **Consumer Analysis** — Ask what "job" your product does for the consumer. Three Rs: Is your claim **Relevant**, does it **Resonate**, is it **Realistic**?
- **Competitive Analysis** — How is your product different and superior? Three Ds: Is your claim **Distinctive**, **Defensible**, **Durable**?
- **Company Analysis** — Three Fs: Is your value proposition **Feasible**, **Favorable**, **Faithful** to the company?

### Metrics Aligned to the Marketing Funnel

| Funnel Stage | Metric | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Awareness | Impressions | Number of times an ad was displayed |
| Awareness | CPM | Cost per thousand impressions |
| Consideration | Clicks | Number of ad clicks |
| Consideration | CTR | Clicks / Impressions |
| Consideration | CPC | Cost per click |
| Conversion | ROAS | Revenue / Ad spend |
| Conversion | ROI | (Revenue × profit margin – Ad spend) / Ad spend |
| Conversion | Conversion Rate | Buyers / Clicks |
| Conversion | CPO | Ad spend / Number of orders |
| Conversion | CAC | Ad spend / Number of new customers |
| Retention | LTV | Long-term profit earned from a customer |

**Key formulas:**
- Revenue = Impressions × CTR × Conversion Rate × Price
- Profit = (Impressions × CTR × Conversion Rate × Margin) – Ad cost
- ROAS = Revenue / Ad Spend
- ROI = (Revenue × Profit Margin – Ad Spend) / Ad Spend

## M3: Acquiring Customers — Paid Media

### Paid, Owned, and Earned Media

- **Paid media** (outbound marketing): Company pays to showcase products in media — TV, search ads, display, social ads.
- **Owned media** (inbound marketing): Company assets — website, blog, social profiles, newsletters. Uses SEO to drive discovery.
- **Earned media**: Organic publicity generated by customers — reviews, social posts, shares. Most authentic but least controllable.

These three types have **synergy**: e.g., paid influencer (earned + paid), boosted organic post (owned + paid), viral owned content (owned + earned).

### Types of Paid Media

**Search Engine Marketing (SEM):**
Best when consumers are actively searching for a product or solution. Five components:
1. Choosing keywords (branded vs. generic/non-branded)
2. Designing ad content
3. Identifying the best landing page
4. Developing a bidding strategy
5. Analyzing results and optimizing

**Display Advertising:**
Targets consumers not actively searching. Types: banner ads, expandable banner ads, interstitial ads, rich media ads.
- Pros: broader reach, retargeting efficiency, creative potential
- Cons: low CTR, consumer displeasure, privacy concerns

**Native Advertising:**
Designed to blend with platform content. Viewed 50%+ more than traditional display. Higher CTR. Challenges: higher production cost, requires editorial skills, raises trust/ethics questions.

**Paid Social:**
Display ads on social media platforms. Strong targeting via user data. Best for multi-funnel objectives and retargeting. Challenge: crowded, increasingly expensive.

**Digital Video / OTT / CTV:**
- **OTT** (Over the Top): Streaming services (Hulu, Netflix, Disney+)
- **CTV** (Connected TV): Smart TVs, Roku, Fire TV, gaming consoles. Enables better targeting and measurement than linear TV.
- Video works at all funnel stages: awareness (brand storytelling), consideration (education), conversion (product demos)

### Paid Media and the Marketing Funnel

Channel selection must be guided by: (1) your objective, (2) your target audience, (3) your value proposition, and (4) which funnel stage you are targeting.

- **Top of funnel**: Create category and brand awareness → display, video, OTT
- **Middle of funnel**: Highlight why to consider your product → paid social, native
- **Bottom of funnel**: Price, design, availability → paid search (highest ROAS/ROI)

### Balancing Brand-Building vs. Performance Marketing

- **Brand-building** (upper funnel): Long-term, sustainable competitive advantage. Hard to measure directly.
- **Performance marketing** (lower funnel): Short-term sales growth. Highly measurable.
- Companies must invest in both. Focusing exclusively on short-term ROAS metrics stunts long-term growth.

## M4: Acquiring Customers — Owned and Earned Media

### Owned Media: Benefits and SEO

Owned media includes: company website, blog, app, social media presence, newsletters, brochures.

Benefits:
- Full control over message
- Sets brand tone, look, and feel
- Builds deeper customer relationships
- Works at all funnel stages
- Greater creativity for brand-building

**Search Engine Optimization (SEO):**
Google ranks organic results by two criteria: **relevance** (content match to search terms) and **authority** (how many other sites link to yours).

The three pillars of SEO:
1. **Technical optimization** — Site accessibility, mobile-first, page speed
2. **Content optimization** — Keyword-rich, relevant content that matches consumer searches
3. **Link optimization** — Earn backlinks from other sites to build authority

**Keyword Research and the Funnel:**
- Top of funnel: Short, 1-2 word, high-volume phrases (broad awareness)
- Middle of funnel: More specific phrases, early purchase intent
- Bottom of funnel: Long-tail, low-volume, high-conversion phrases (e.g., "deals on X near me in March")

Strategy: Start at the bottom (less competition, higher conversion) and work up.

### Content Strategy

Content must be tied to target audience and value proposition. Key questions when creating content:
1. Who is the target audience?
2. What problem are you solving?
3. What makes you unique (your value proposition)?
4. What format best tells your story? (blog, video, podcast, image, testimonial)
5. Which channels will you publish on?
6. How will you refresh, measure, and optimize?

### Email Marketing

Email works at all funnel stages: lead generation (top), cart abandonment recovery (middle), re-purchase (bottom).

Benefits:
- Cost-effective — no ad platform intermediary
- Full message control
- Enables calls to action (CTAs)
- Drives consideration and purchase

### Earned Media: Monitor, Participate, Leverage, Manage, Measure

Companies engage with earned media in five ways:

1. **Monitor**: Observe consumer attitudes on social media. Real-time gauge of brand perception.
2. **Participate**: Actively join earned media conversations. React to current events; produce marketing material organically.
3. **Leverage**: Amplify positive messages. Understand why consumers share content to create shareable messages.
4. **Manage**: Have strong brand advocates as defense against negative commentary. Respond quickly to negative reviews.
5. **Measure**: Most companies track views, likes, shares — converted to "earned media value" (equivalent paid media cost).

Challenges to measuring earned media:
- **Self-selection bias**: Vocal users (lovers/haters) are not representative of the silent majority
- **Homophily**: Similar people cluster together, inflating apparent reach

### Influencer Marketing

**Tiers by follower count:**
- **Macro influencers** (large following): Best for top-of-funnel awareness campaigns. Broad reach, less targeted, higher cost.
- **Mid-tier / Micro influencers** (smaller, engaged following): Higher influence per follower. Best for consideration and engagement goals.
- **Unpaid influencers**: Organic advocates — highly authentic.

**Factors to evaluate in influencer partnerships:**
Number of followers, authenticity of endorsements, follower engagement, posting consistency, content quality, alignment to brand values, audience relevance, exclusivity.

**Risks:** Fake followers, inconsistency with brand values, lack of control over behavior, legal/disclosure issues.

**Compensation:** Price based on reach (followers), then adjusted for engagement rate, performance (CPC), and competitive landscape. Consider non-monetary value (social capital, product access).

**Measurement:**
- Awareness: reach of shared media, brand perception surveys
- Engagement: comments, website traffic, organic searches, time on site
- Conversion: purchase data, affiliate links

## M5: Engaging Customers

### Retention and Personalization

Acquiring a new customer costs far more than retaining an existing one. Retention is the key driver of **Customer Lifetime Value (LTV)**.

- **Retention rate**: % of customers who stay in a given period
- **Churn rate**: % of customers who leave (Churn = 100 – Retention)

**Personalization** reduces churn by aligning products and experiences to individual preferences. Most valuable when:
- The product/service is difficult to describe
- Variety is large
- Product differences are subjective or abstract (wine, fashion, beauty, restaurants, movies)

**AI Techniques for Personalization:**
- **Natural Language Processing (NLP)**: Analyzes consumer search language
- **Collaborative filtering**: "Users like you also bought" — predicts preferences from behavioral overlap
- **Content-based filtering**: "Similar to what you viewed" — uses product tags/features to generate recommendations

### From Storytelling to Story-making

- **Storytelling**: Company crafts a brand narrative to engage customers (vs. hard sell)
- **Story-making**: Customer becomes an active participant in creating the brand story. Companies design experiences that involve customers in the brand.

### Mastercard's Digital Engine (7-Step Engagement Framework)

1. **Emotional Spark** — Create excitement; inspire consumers to generate their own stories
2. **Engagement** — Encourage target audience to share their stories
3. **Offers** — Use macro and micro analysis to identify the right offers for consumers
4. **Optimization** — Test and refine creative content, copy, and campaign themes
5. **Amplification** — Put marketing dollars behind proven, effective content
6. **Network Effects** — Share consumer stories on social; encourage peer-to-peer sharing
7. **Incremental Transactions** — Measure effectiveness via reach, engagement, qualified leads, and incremental sales (transactions that would not have happened without the campaign)

### Brand Communities and Engagement

Companies build brand communities to:
- Enhance brand-consumer connection
- Build brand equity and loyalty
- Generate authentic advocacy

Consumers join brand communities for:
- Sense of belonging ("we-ness")
- Sense of responsibility and helping others
- Meaningful rituals and traditions

**Types of brand communities:**
- **Consumer-initiated**: Organic fan clubs, highly authentic
- **Brand-created**: Brand launches a meeting place (online or offline) for consumers to engage with each other and the brand

**Brand communities and the funnel:**
- Top: Brand awareness and brand knowledge
- Middle: Active engagement and participation
- Bottom: Conversion — done by listening to community, thanking them, building products from their input (not a hard sales pitch)

## M6: Allocating Budget and Measuring Success

### Key Budget Allocation Metrics

**CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost)**
- Formula: Total marketing spend / Number of new customers
- Limitation: Does not account for revenue generated or customer quality. Lowest CAC channel may attract price-sensitive or low-value customers.

**ROI (Return on Investment)**
- Formula: ROI = (Profit – Ad Spend) / Ad Spend
- Measures profit (not revenue) per marketing dollar

**ROAS (Return on Ad Spend)**
- Formula: ROAS = Revenue / Ad Spend
- Paid search typically has the highest ROAS (captures bottom-funnel ready buyers)
- Display/video have lower ROAS but drive upper-funnel awareness that enables search to convert

### Six Factors in Budget Allocation Decisions

1. **Potential volume from the channel** — What proportion of your audience is actually reachable there?
2. **Desire to learn by experimentation** — Some channels need investment even without proven ROI yet
3. **Quality of lead** — Low CAC doesn't matter if those customers have high churn or low spend
4. **Marginal cost, not average cost** — CAC increases with scale; diminishing returns are real
5. **Goals** — Short-term sales may not be the only objective; awareness and brand-building matter
6. **Attribution** — Without it, you over-credit last-touch (search) and under-invest in upper-funnel channels

### Attribution Models

| Model | How it Works | Best For |
|---|---|---|
| First interaction (first touch) | 100% credit to first ad exposure | Valuing brand awareness |
| Last interaction (last touch) | 100% credit to last ad seen | Conversion-focused analysis |
| Linear | Equal credit to all touchpoints | Balanced view |
| Time decay | More credit to recent touchpoints | Short purchase cycles |
| Position-based (U-shaped) | More weight to first and last touchpoints | Balanced awareness + conversion |
| Experiment-based (A/B) | Uses controlled tests to isolate ad impact | Most accurate, most resource-intensive |
| Econometric models | Regression-based, estimates marginal effect of each channel | Enterprise-level, cross-channel view |

### Customer Lifetime Value (LTV)

Spending to acquire a customer is an investment — justified if the customer makes multiple future purchases.

**Customer Value Grid (2x2 matrix):**
- **Star customers**: High profitability + High satisfaction → Protect and grow
- **Vulnerable customers**: High profitability + Low satisfaction → At risk; improve service
- **Free riders**: Low profitability + High satisfaction → Taking discounts; not profitable
- **Lost causes**: Low profitability + Low satisfaction → Minimize investment

**LTV Formulas:**

Simple:
```
T = 1 / churn rate   (expected customer lifetime in years)
LTV = m × T          (m = annual contribution margin)
```

With time value of money:
```
LTV = m / (1 - r + i) = margin / (churn + discount rate)
```
Where r = annual retention rate, i = discount rate

**LTV-CAC Relationship:**
```
ROI = (LTV / CAC) – 1
```
A healthy LTV:CAC ratio is typically 3:1 or better.

### Key Drivers of LTV

| Driver | Strategy |
|---|---|
| Customer acquisition | Acquire customers more likely to provide high value and low churn |
| Customer retention | Identify why customers churn; target specific defector types with tailored tactics |
| Customer development | Increase share of wallet via cross-sell, upsell, or adjacent product expansion |

### Fixed vs. Variable Investments

- **Fixed investments**: Long-term; improve conversion rates at a specific funnel stage (e.g., landing page optimization). Make as early as possible; effects compound over time.
- **Variable investments**: Short-term effects on quarterly KPIs (e.g., awareness spend drives traffic in that quarter). Subject to diminishing returns.

### Budget Allocation Principles

1. Understand which funnel stage has the biggest "leak" — allocate to fix the bottleneck
2. Not all funnel stages respond equally to additional spend
3. Balance short-term performance marketing with long-term brand investment
4. Test and learn constantly — there is no universal formula; optimize for your specific product and audience
5. Use data-driven attribution models (Adobe DDOM approach: anchor the entire organization in the customer journey, with unified KPIs across departments)
